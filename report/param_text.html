All three trends reported in Sec. III-D of the paper are reproduced. Coupling gap C: narrowing C from 0.4 to 0.2 mm raises the coupling capacitance and pulls the lower resonance down to 2.405 GHz — right on the paper's 2.4 GHz — and the upper resonance down to 4.56 GHz, but it weakens the lower-band match (−6 dB). Widening C to 0.8 mm raises the upper resonance to 4.88 GHz and improves the lower-band match (−14.3 dB), while the lower resonance stays at 2.55 GHz. So C trades frequency against matching, and is the main reason our nominal lower band sits about 4 % above the paper's. Permittivity: going from εr = 4.0 to 4.6 moves both bands down (2.595 → 2.50 GHz and 4.815 → 4.695 GHz), as the larger εeff shortens λg. This also matters in practice, because the permittivity of commercial FR4 varies between about 4.2 and 4.7. Substrate thickness: increasing h from 1.2 to 2.0 mm lowers both resonances in the same way (2.605 → 2.515 GHz and 4.815 → 4.67 GHz), because more of the field lies inside the dielectric.
